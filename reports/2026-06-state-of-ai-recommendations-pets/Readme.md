# The State of AI Recommendations in Pets

**Recommendation Intelligence Research™ · Atom Foundry · June 2026**

## Research Summary

We analyzed 4,000 AI-generated pet product recommendations across 20 high-intent shopping prompts to determine whether AI systems recommend brands because their stores are AI-ready.

Unlike previous categories, Pets produced a stronger result.

Across 400 prompt runs, 405 distinct brands, and thousands of recommendations, Recommendation Frequency™ showed a weak negative relationship with AI Commerce Score™.

**Correlation between Recommendation Frequency™ and AI Commerce Score™**

**r = -0.366**

This is the strongest relationship observed so far and suggests that more frequently recommended brands often have weaker stores.

---

## Key Findings

* 4,000 recommendations analyzed
* 405 distinct brands identified
* Recommendation Frequency™ and AI Commerce Score™ showed a weak negative relationship
* Highly recommended pet brands frequently had poor AI readiness
* Strong AI-ready stores often received little recommendation visibility
* Legacy pet brands dominated recommendations despite weak store quality

## Notable Observation

Blue Buffalo appeared in **27.8%** of prompt runs despite scoring only **30/100** in AI Commerce Score™.

Merrick appeared in **24.8%** of prompts while scoring only **42/100**.

Meanwhile, some of the strongest stores in the category, including Benebone, Casper, and Pawstruck, received very little recommendation visibility despite substantially higher readiness scores.

The results suggest that AI recommendation behavior in Pets is driven primarily by brand familiarity rather than store readiness.

---

## Conclusion

Pets produced the clearest result observed in the Recommendation Intelligence™ research series.

Recommendation Frequency™ showed a weak negative relationship with AI Commerce Score™, suggesting that highly recommended brands often have weaker stores.

Across four independent categories and more than 16,000 recommendations analyzed, recommendation frequency has never shown a meaningful positive relationship with store readiness.

The evidence increasingly suggests that current AI systems recommend familiar brands rather than the stores they can most effectively read, understand, and evaluate.
