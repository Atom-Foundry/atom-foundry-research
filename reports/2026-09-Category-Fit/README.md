# Authority Adds 9 Points in Trust Categories. Familiarity Adds 14.

**Recommendation Intelligence Research™ · Study #39**

## Abstract

PDP Specificity found that specific claims help functional categories and cost trust categories. This study asks whether the same category split appears for authority and brand familiarity.

We reran both signals across the same eight-brand category axis: four functional categories and four trust and safety sensitive categories, with no rating in the room.

Across two independent rounds, authority produced a 74.3% follow-the-signal rate in trust categories versus 65.2% in functional categories, a +9.1 percentage-point difference. Familiarity produced a 74.0% follow-the-signal rate in trust categories versus 60.2% in functional categories, a +13.8 percentage-point difference.

Both category × signal-level interaction tests replicated in the same direction in both independent rounds.

## Experimental scope

- 8 real brands
- 4 functional categories
- 4 trust and safety sensitive categories
- 20 purchase intents
- 5 repeats per condition
- 2 independent rounds
- 3,200 calls per round
- 6,400 calls total, plus matched judge calls
- gpt-4o
- forced two-way brand choice
- no rating in the room

## Combined results

| Signal | Functional | Trust / safety sensitive | Trust minus functional |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Authority | 65.2% | 74.3% | +9.1 pp |
| Familiarity | 60.2% | 74.0% | +13.8 pp |

## Round-level results

- Authority: functional 65.4% → 64.9%; trust 74.5% → 74.0%.
- Familiarity: functional 60.0% → 60.4%; trust 74.4% → 73.5%.

## Interpretation

The category split runs in the opposite direction from PDP Specificity. In this study, authority and familiarity moved the recommended brand more in trust and safety sensitive categories than in functional categories.

The finding supports a narrower conclusion: the effect of a recommendation signal can depend on the type of decision represented by the category. It does not establish a universal hierarchy of signals across all ecommerce categories.

## Limitations

The study uses eight brands, one model, a forced-choice setup, and a simplified functional-versus-trust category axis. Brand-level effects are heterogeneous beneath the category averages. The comparison with specificity comes from a separate study with a different brand set rather than one unified experiment.
