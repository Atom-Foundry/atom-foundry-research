# AI Recommends by Fame, Not by Store Quality

**Recommendation Intelligence Research™ · Atom Foundry · June 2026**

## Research Summary

We analyzed the 200 most-recommended brands across five ecommerce categories to determine what actually explains AI recommendation behavior.

The answer was not store quality.

Public fame signals explained substantially more recommendation behavior than AI Commerce Score™.

However, even fame explained only a fraction of the outcome.

## Key Findings

* 200 brands analyzed
* 24.9% of recommendation behavior explained by fame
* 2.1% explained by store quality
* Fame explained approximately 12 times more recommendation behavior than AI Commerce Score™
* Approximately 75% of recommendation behavior remained unexplained

---

## Core Results

### Store Quality

AI Commerce Score™

**R² = 0.021**

Store readiness explained approximately 2.1% of recommendation behavior.

---

### Public Fame Signals

Wikipedia pageviews, language coverage, article size, and brand nameability

**R² = 0.249**

Public fame signals explained approximately 24.9% of recommendation behavior.

---

## Top Finding

The most-recommended brands and the least-recommended brands had nearly identical store quality.

| Metric                     | Top 50 Brands | Bottom 50 Brands |
| -------------------------- | ------------: | ---------------: |
| Average AI Commerce Score™ |          50.8 |             49.8 |
| Has Wikipedia Article      |           56% |              28% |

The difference was not store quality.

The difference was fame.

The most-recommended brands were approximately twice as likely to possess public fame signals.

---

## Recommendation Stability

A common assumption is that AI recommendations are random.

The data suggests otherwise.

Top recommendation consistency:

| Category      | Top-1 Stability |
| ------------- | --------------: |
| Supplements   |             91% |
| Coffee        |             86% |
| Pets          |             85% |
| Beauty        |             84% |
| Home & Living |             78% |

The same brands repeatedly occupied the same recommendation positions.

The hierarchy appears stable rather than random.

---

## Interpretation

Store quality matters surprisingly little.

Public fame signals matter significantly more.

Yet even fame explains only about one quarter of recommendation behavior.

Most recommendation behavior remains hidden inside model training data, proprietary weighting systems, retrieval logic, and ranking mechanisms that cannot be directly observed.

This suggests that current AI recommendation systems operate much closer to fame engines than quality engines.

---

## Conclusion

Three findings emerged.

* Store quality explained very little recommendation behavior.
* Fame explained substantially more than store quality.
* Recommendation outcomes were highly stable across repeated runs.

The most important finding is not that fame matters.

It is that most recommendation behavior remains unexplained.

Understanding that hidden layer is the purpose of Recommendation Intelligence™.
