# The State of AI Recommendations in Home & Living

**Recommendation Intelligence Research™ · Atom Foundry · June 2026**

## Research Summary

We analyzed 4,000 AI-generated home and living recommendations across 20 high-intent shopping prompts to determine whether AI systems recommend brands because their stores are AI-ready.

The answer was once again no.

Across 400 prompt runs, 271 distinct brands, and thousands of recommendations, Recommendation Frequency™ showed virtually no relationship with AI Commerce Score™.

**Correlation between Recommendation Frequency™ and AI Commerce Score™**

**r = 0.108**

This is statistically indistinguishable from zero.

Home & Living became the fifth and final category in the Recommendation Intelligence™ series and replicated the same pattern observed throughout the research.

---

## Key Findings

* 4,000 recommendations analyzed
* 271 distinct brands identified
* Recommendation Frequency™ and AI Commerce Score™ showed no measurable relationship
* Famous home brands dominated recommendations
* Highly optimized stores often received little recommendation visibility
* Marketplace and retailer influence was higher than in any other category

---

## Top Recommended Brands

* IKEA
* West Elm
* Pottery Barn
* Brooklinen
* Parachute

---

## Notable Observation

IKEA appeared in **48.3%** of prompt runs while scoring **64/100**.

West Elm appeared in **47.0%** of prompts while scoring **60/100**.

Meanwhile, some of the strongest stores in the category, including Article, Burrow, Uplift Desk, and GhostBed, received very little recommendation visibility despite substantially higher readiness scores.

The results suggest that recommendation behavior remains driven by brand familiarity rather than AI readiness.

---

## Conclusion

Home & Living replicated the findings observed across Beauty, Supplements, and Coffee.

Recommendation Frequency™ showed no meaningful relationship with AI Commerce Score™ or store readiness.

Across five independent categories and more than 20,000 recommendations analyzed, recommendation frequency never demonstrated a meaningful positive relationship with store readiness.

The evidence now supports a broader Recommendation Intelligence™ hypothesis: AI systems currently recommend familiar brands rather than the most machine-readable stores.
