# The State of AI Recommendations in Supplements

**Recommendation Intelligence Research™ · Atom Foundry · June 2026**

## Research Summary

We analyzed 4,000 AI-generated supplement recommendations across 20 high-intent shopping prompts to determine whether AI systems recommend brands because their stores are AI-ready.

The answer was no.

Across 400 prompt runs, 371 distinct brands, and thousands of recommendations, Recommendation Frequency™ showed virtually no relationship with AI Commerce Score™.

**Correlation between Recommendation Frequency™ and AI Commerce Score™**

**r = -0.015**

This is statistically indistinguishable from zero.

The most recommended supplement brands were often stores with poor AI readiness, while several of the strongest stores received little recommendation visibility.

---

## Key Findings

* 4,000 recommendations analyzed
* 371 distinct brands identified
* Recommendation Frequency™ and AI Commerce Score™ showed no measurable relationship
* Highly recommended brands frequently had poor AI readiness
* Highly optimized stores often received little recommendation visibility
* AI recommendation behavior appeared driven more by brand familiarity than store readiness

## Notable Observation

NOW Foods appeared in **30.3%** of prompt runs despite scoring only **14/100** in AI Commerce Score™, placing it deep in AI Invisible Risk territory.

Nature's Way showed a similar pattern, appearing frequently while also scoring **14/100**.

Meanwhile, some of the strongest stores in the category, including Liquid I.V., Moon Juice, and DripDrop, received little recommendation visibility despite substantially higher readiness scores.

The results suggest that current AI recommendation systems rely more heavily on brand familiarity and historical presence than on actual store readiness.

---

## Conclusion

Supplements replicated the pattern first observed in Beauty.

Recommendation Frequency™ showed no meaningful relationship with AI Commerce Score™ or store readiness.

The evidence suggests that AI systems currently recommend brands they already know rather than the stores that are easiest to read, understand, and evaluate.

Across two independent categories and 8,000 recommendations analyzed, the same signal emerged: visibility, recommendation, and readiness appear to be separate phenomena.
