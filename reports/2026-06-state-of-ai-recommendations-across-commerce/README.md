# The State of AI Recommendations Across Commerce 2026

**Recommendation Intelligence Research™ · Atom Foundry · June 2026**

## Research Summary

We analyzed 20,000 AI-generated product recommendations across five ecommerce categories to determine whether AI systems recommend brands because their stores are AI-ready.

The answer was consistently no.

Across 100 shopping intents, 1,490 distinct brands, and five independent categories, Recommendation Frequency™ showed no meaningful positive relationship with AI Commerce Score™.

The same pattern appeared repeatedly regardless of category.

---

## Key Findings

* 20,000 recommendations analyzed
* 1,490 distinct brands identified
* 5 ecommerce categories studied
* 100 shopping intents analyzed
* Recommendation Frequency™ showed no meaningful positive relationship with AI Commerce Score™ in any category
* Marketplace recommendations increased significantly in more commoditized categories
* Brand familiarity consistently outperformed store readiness

---

## Correlation Results

| Beauty        |           4,000 |           0.170 |

| Coffee        |           4,000 |           0.019 |
| Pets          |           4,000 |          -0.366 |
| Home & Living |           4,000 |           0.108 |

Across all five categories, Recommendation Frequency™ never demonstrated a meaningful positive relationship with AI Commerce Score™.

Three categories produced effectively zero correlation. Home & Living replicated the same result. Pets produced the only statistically meaningful relationship and it was negative.

## Category Comparison

| Pets          |    405 |    19.8% |              7.1% |
| Home & Living |    271 |    60.7% |             20.9% |

A second pattern emerged across the dataset.

The more commoditized and everyday the category became, the more often AI deferred recommendations to retailers and marketplaces instead of naming individual brands.

Beauty remained overwhelmingly brand-driven.

Home & Living produced the highest marketplace share, with more than one in five recommendations going to retailers rather than brands.

---

## Recommendation by Memory™

The strongest pattern across all five categories was not store quality.

It was brand familiarity.

The brands AI recommended most frequently were typically the brands most likely to have been encountered during training.

Examples included:

* Clinique and SkinCeuticals in Beauty
* NOW Foods in Supplements
* Peet's Coffee in Coffee
* Blue Buffalo and Purina in Pets
* IKEA, West Elm, and Pottery Barn in Home & Living

Many of these brands possessed only average AI Commerce Scores™.

Several were not present in the store index at all.

We refer to this pattern as **Recommendation by Memory™**.

The alternative is **Recommendation by Understanding™**, where an AI system retrieves, reads, evaluates, and compares stores before making a recommendation.

Current recommendation behavior appears heavily weighted toward memory rather than understanding.

---

## Conclusion

Across five ecommerce categories and 20,000 AI recommendations, store readiness did not predict recommendation frequency.

The findings suggest that current AI systems primarily recommend brands they already know rather than the stores they can most effectively read, understand, and evaluate.

Visibility is not recommendation.

Recommendation is not readiness.

Understanding why AI chooses one brand over another remains one of the most important unanswered questions in AI commerce.
